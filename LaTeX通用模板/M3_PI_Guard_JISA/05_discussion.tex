\section{Discussion}

\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Existing evidence changes the research claim}

\label{sec:discussion-existing-evidence-changes-the-research-claim}

The completed medium and Public experiments do not support presenting the full configuration package as stronger than the policy package. In the 5,082-record DeepSeek V4 Pro Public matrix, the full package produced more unsafe effects (38/600 versus 13/600), while both packages completed 49/168 benign tool tasks. The 1,791-record V4 Flash matrix showed the same security direction: 24/400 unsafe effects for the full package versus 12/400 for the policy package. The Flash utility difference was small (38/145 versus 35/145).

This is scientifically useful negative evidence, but it was originally package-level evidence. E1 resolves the prompt/runtime confounding by crossing the prompt profile with the module bundle in one execution window. Its module, prompt, and interaction estimates were all small, and none supplied evidence of a positive safety or utility contribution. The result identifies the prompt factor separately from the runtime bundle; it still cannot separate provenance, temporal/data-flow, monotonicity, and confirmation from one another. It shows that the evaluated bundle did not earn a clear advantage under these tasks, not that any individual mechanism is intrinsically ineffective.

\subsection{Authorization is part of the treatment}

\label{sec:discussion-authorization-is-part-of-the-treatment}

Authorization is often treated as fixed infrastructure rather than an experimental variable. That assumption is risky in tool-agent evaluation. An exact per-case authorization object reconstructed from the expected answer can remove uncertainty that a deployed Guard must resolve. A broad policy can improve apparent utility by authorizing too much. An underspecified policy can make safe completion impossible, while conflicting constraints may increase denial, confirmation, or failure even when they prevent unsafe effects.

The paired design makes these trade-offs observable. \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} is the primary deployment-like regime because it is created from trusted user intent before untrusted context is processed. \nolinkurl{exact_gold} is an oracle sensitivity condition, not a preferred policy source. The \nolinkurl{broad}, \nolinkurl{underspecified}, and \nolinkurl{conflicting} cells are stress tests on a fixed nested sample. Their value lies in showing whether a headline security number is stable to plausible policy construction errors.

The DeepSeek paired experiment did not reveal an unsafe-effect advantage for \nolinkurl{exact_gold}: three of four primary cells had 0/100 unsafe effects, while the remaining policy-package \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} cell had 1/100. The meaningful movement was instead in utility and runtime burden. Moving from \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} to \nolinkurl{exact_gold} reduced pooled benign-tool completion by 21.67 points for the policy package and 15.00 points for the full package. The task-cluster bootstrap intervals excluded zero, although the corresponding McNemar comparisons did not remain below 0.05 after Holm correction.

The pooled direction is incomplete. AgentDojo completion increased by 13.33 points for the policy package and 10.00 points for the full package, whereas M3-v2 completion fell by 56.67 and 40.00 points. Authorization semantics and benchmark task structure therefore interact strongly enough that a single pooled oracle-versus-compiled estimate should not be transported to either benchmark in isolation.

The operational shift was substantial. Exact policies eliminated ASK in both packages but increased the share of records with DENY from 7.22\% to 48.89\% for the policy package and from 11.11\% to 45.00\% for the full package. Failure rose to roughly 24\% and p95 latency to roughly 19.5 seconds in both exact cells. The broad, conflicting, and underspecified stress regimes likewise reduced completion by 25--30 points on their nested benign-tool subset without changing the observed attack floor. These findings show that policy specification can move burden and utility even when a headline attack rate appears stable.

The pessimistic \nolinkurl{unsafe_effect OR operational_failure} bound makes that point more explicit. Exact authorization increased this attack endpoint by 18 points for both packages overall, driven by +36-point changes on AgentDojo and no change on M3-v2. This does not convert failures into attacks; it demonstrates that a near-zero observed unsafe-effect rate can coexist with poor operational robustness.

\subsection{Provenance sensitivity exists at the decision layer}

\label{sec:discussion-provenance-sensitivity-exists-at-the-decision-layer}

The controlled source-authority experiment sharpens the negative package result. Holding proposition, value, position, and policy fixed, the policy-only Guard produced the same deterministic decision distribution under trusted and untrusted origins. The full Guard changed its untrusted-origin distribution to 30 ALLOW, 30 ASK, and 60 DENY. Across 240 un\-au\-thor\-ized proposals there were no false allows; across 240 authorized proposals there were 30 false blocks. This is direct evidence that the implemented full Guard is provenance-sensitive at its deterministic decision boundary.

That mechanism did not translate into completed end-to-end actions. All 240 model--Guard runs executed zero actions: trusted contexts were routed almost entirely to ASK, while full/untrusted contexts were predominantly DENY. The correct interpretation therefore has two layers. Offline replay establishes mechanism behavior; end-to-end execution reveals a confirmation bottleneck and cannot be replaced by the replay's decision accuracy. A provenance mechanism can be logically active while the surrounding interaction design supplies little usable utility.

\subsection{Executed effects and utility must be reported together}

\label{sec:discussion-executed-effects-and-utility-must-be-reported-together}

A low prompt-injection success rate is not sufficient evidence of a useful runtime defense. A detector may flag adversarial text without preventing an effect; a runtime may prevent effects by denying nearly every useful action; and a parser/provider failure may look secure if the evaluator counts only successful executions. We therefore treat isolated executed effect as the security endpoint and benign tool-task completion as a joint primary endpoint.

The Public evidence already illustrates the problem. Relative to the structural condition, both M3 variants reduced unsafe effects but also had much lower benign-tool completion. The policy package then outperformed the full package on security without losing additional utility. The interpretation is not that provenance is intrinsically harmful. It is that the evaluated bundle did not earn a security advantage under these tasks and policies.

ASK, DENY, operational failure, latency, tokens, and cost provide the missing system-facing context. An authorization regime that shifts unsafe effects downward but greatly increases ASK may be acceptable in high-consequence workflows and unacceptable in routine automation. A regime that shifts both ASK and failure upward may simply externalize uncertainty rather than resolve it. The paper reports these outcomes separately. The unsafe-or-failure sensitivity endpoint is shown only as an explicitly pessimistic robustness bound, not as a replacement composite score.

\subsection{Adaptive evidence is budget-sensitive and conditionally extended}

\label{sec:discussion-adaptive-evidence-is-budget-sensitive-and-conditionally-extended}

The 40-task defense-aware panel found first unsafe effect for 4/40 tasks by 50 queries and 18/40 by 200 queries. Thus a budget-50 evaluation would have missed most successes later observed by budget 200. This supports the general warning that a static or weak attacker can overstate robustness \citep{L27}.

The complete-panel evidence stops at 200. E5 extends only 12 prespecified B200 survivors to 500 and stops each task at first unsafe effect. No new first success occurred (0/12; Wilson 95\% upper limit 24.25\%). This conditional tail can estimate \nolinkurl{P(first success by 500 | no success by 200)} for the selected survivors, but it cannot be merged with the original 40 tasks into a nominal full B500 curve, establish saturation, estimate an asymptotic attack rate, or support a hardest-task-2,000 claim. The null point estimate does not establish zero tail risk because the interval remains wide and the cohort is selected conditional on surviving budget 200.

\subsection{Contemporaneous calibration restores an active control}

\label{sec:discussion-contemporaneous-calibration-restores-an-active-control}

The near-zero E1 and E3 attack endpoints raise a calibration question: were the tested attacks inactive at the time of execution, or did defended conditions actually differ from an active control? E6 addresses this in a separately executed historically risk-enriched cohort, but it is not an independent sample validation: 47/60 tasks are also E3 anchors. No defense produced 17/60 unsafe effects, including events in both AgentDojo and M3-v2, while policy/normal produced 2/60 and full/safe produced 0/60. Attack activity was therefore observed within the selected contemporaneous block; the original multi-window activity gate was neither satisfied nor tested.

This calibration does not rescue a broad defense-superiority claim. The cohort was selected from historical control-positive cases, contains no benign tasks, and was run in one execution block. It estimates neither deployment prevalence nor utility or temporal repeatability. Operational failures were also material: 12/60 under no defense and 3/60 even under full/safe. The VIGIL contrast was heterogeneous, moving in opposite directions across the two benchmarks. E6 is therefore best read as an active-control check plus a conditional comparison, not a general robustness ranking or an individual provenance-module ablation.

\subsection{Relation to recent provenance and authorization work}

\label{sec:discussion-relation-to-recent-provenance-and-authorization-work}

VIGIL, DRIFT, the Task Shield, IPIGuard, Progent, CaMeL, ARGUS, AuthGraph, FAVA, and provenance-sensitivity auditing occupy overlapping parts of the design space \citep{L01,L02,L03,L21,L22,L24,L25,L26,L35,L36}. These works rule out claims that M3 is the first verify-before-commit system, the first parameter-source defense, or the first structured runtime authorizer.

The remaining contribution is methodological and empirical. The study holds task content fixed while changing authorization regimes, separately manipulates prompt and runtime factors, and performs a source-only counterfactual. It keeps evaluator fields offline, measures post-enforcement effects, retains failure in ITT, and reports operational burden. In particular, the recent provenance-sensitivity study changes source authority in controlled next-action tasks \citep{L35}. The present design adds deterministic Guard replay and complete tool trajectories while keeping those two evidence layers separate.

\subsection{Cross-provider interpretation}

\label{sec:discussion-cross-provider-interpretation}

Two distinct Gemini attempts remain diagnostic. The earlier authorization study closed with 32/450 terminal work items, including only five with official model responses; 83 observed items remained nonterminal and 335 were unstarted. The separately executed E6 prefix then stopped after five response-bearing records and one response-less VIGIL dependency failure; 174 untouched plan rows were closed administratively. Administrative closure is not scientific observation.

Neither dataset meets a paired-coverage gate. In E6, comparisons involving VIGIL have 0/30 minimum response-complete pairs. We therefore report all Gemini effects as not estimable rather than plotting a zero, ranking providers, or combining the two incompatible samples. External validity remains limited to DeepSeek V4 Pro and V4 Flash.

\subsection{Limitations}

\label{sec:discussion-limitations}

First, the new primary experiment uses one proprietary model and two benchmark families; the larger Public evidence adds another model from the same provider, not an independent vendor. Model updates, tool schemas, system prompts, and application context may change the effects. Both Gemini attempts closed incomplete and contribute no cross-provider inference. Although execution dates, official response identifiers, and returned model names are retained, provider-managed aliases do not make the underlying weights immutable.

Second, the new authorization experiment has a strong attack-endpoint floor: only one unsafe effect occurred across the 400 primary attack records. It can therefore estimate utility and operational burden more informatively than small security differences between its four primary cells. Security-direction evidence comes mainly from the larger Public matrices and must not be inferred from these near-zero cells alone.

Third, E1 separates prompt profile from the runtime module bundle, but the full module factor still combines provenance, data-flow, monotonicity, and confirmation. It identifies a bundle-level module effect, not the contribution of each internal mechanism. E2 isolates source authority at the input boundary, yet confirmation and blocking create an end-to-end action floor.

Fourth, \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} is one policy compiler, not a complete model of human authorization. The stress regimes approximate common policy errors but do not measure their prevalence in deployed systems. \nolinkurl{exact_gold} is intentionally artificial and should not be read as a recommended configuration.

Fifth, the effect environment is isolated. This is necessary to prevent real email, payment, browser, code, or production actions, but it may omit latency, state, and recovery behavior found in live applications.

Sixth, only a prespecified subset of B200 survivors is extended toward 500. That conditional tail does not constitute a complete B500 panel and cannot prove saturation. Attack search is still one family of defense-aware optimization; another optimizer may find different failures.

Seventh, E6 uses a historically risk-enriched cohort in a single execution block, with 47/60 tasks overlapping E3. It observes activity in that selected block but cannot estimate deployment prevalence, benign utility, cross-date repeatability, or independent-sample replication. Its M3 factor estimates remain bundle-level and do not identify provenance as the causal component.

Eighth, failure handling is conservative but not interpretation-free. All response-bearing failures are terminal in ITT, whereas response-less transport failures can receive bounded explicit recovery. We report raw failures and superseded attempts so readers can assess that choice.

Ninth, \nolinkurl{ASK} is observed only as system confirmation-request incidence. Confirmation stayed pending in the synthetic environment, so this study does not measure human response quality, workload, or usable-security outcomes.

Finally, this paper does not include an independent blind set. It also does not claim first, only, or SOTA status. Its external-validity claims are limited to the models, benchmarks, policy compiler, stress regimes, and attack budgets actually evaluated.

\subsection{Practical implications}

\label{sec:discussion-practical-implications}

Four practical recommendations follow. First, agent-security benchmarks should publish the fields visible to the online Guard and keep evaluator-only answers outside that projection. Second, runtime defenses should be evaluated factorially against a simpler policy package so prompt and module effects are separately identifiable; the internal modules require further ablations before individual attribution. Third, source-only counterfactuals should be paired with both deterministic Guard replay and end-to-end execution, rather than treating either as a proxy for the other. Fourth, security reports should include benign completion, system confirmation/blocking incidence, failures, latency, and attack budget alongside unsafe-effect rate.

The DeepSeek authorization-regime results now tie these recommendations to observed effects: exact and stressed policies materially changed completion, blocking, failure, and latency while observed unsafe effects remained near zero. E6 additionally shows why an active no-defense control should accompany floor-limited defended cells. Both Gemini attempts closed with incomplete diagnostic subsets and cannot alter or broaden the DeepSeek findings.
