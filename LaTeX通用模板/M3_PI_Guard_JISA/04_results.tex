\subsection{Corrected authorization and contemporaneous calibration}

\label{sec:results-corrected-authorization-and-contemporaneous-calibration}

The corrected authorization study records all 900 planned assignments over 180 fixed cases representing 153 tasks. Tables 2--4 summarize corrected authorization, contemporaneous calibration, and bounded component controls, respectively. Paired intervals below are distinct from the tables' marginal Wilson intervals. Of the 900 workflows, 795 end normally, 60 exhaust the step budget, and 45 have parse failures. Across 490 attack assignments, security events are zero under the benchmark-specific checks. With no unguarded control in this study, these observations establish an endpoint floor rather than a protection contrast.

For AgentDojo benign-tool tasks, compiled authorization completes 2/30 under both policy and full modules. Exact-target authorization completes 13/30 under policy and 11/30 under full modules. Paired exact-minus-compiled differences are +36.67 percentage points (95\% CI +18.60 to +53.31; Holm p=0.0039) and +30.00 points (+13.23 to +46.75; Holm p=0.0117), respectively. For M3-v2, compiled counts are 16/30 and 17/30, compared with exact-target counts of 14/30 and 16/30. Their differences, \ensuremath{-}6.67 and \ensuremath{-}3.33 points, have intervals spanning zero. The completion gain was supported on AgentDojo, while the M3-v2 differences remained uncertain.

The separately executed calibration uses 120 cases nested within the authorization cohort and closes all 480 assignments. Of these, 443 workflows end normally, nine exhaust steps, 25 have parse failures, two have runner failures, and one has a provider/stop failure. On AgentDojo, no research Guard yields 3/30 security events and 17/30 benign-tool completions. Policy and full modules each yield 0/30 and 2/30. The paired completion difference is \ensuremath{-}50.00 points (95\% CI \ensuremath{-}66.83 to \ensuremath{-}26.80; Holm p=0.0008), whereas the security difference is \ensuremath{-}10.00 points (\ensuremath{-}25.62 to +3.10; Holm p=0.7500). The utility cost is observable; a security improvement is not established. No-research-Guard, policy, and full M3-v2 security cells are all zero, with benign completions of 16/30, 17/30, and 16/30. Full modules show no identifiable independent end-to-end advantage in these conditions.

Across calibration, seven known security-positive assignments and four unknown outcomes are retained. Configured VIGIL has four AgentDojo attack positives among 28 known outcomes and two unknown attack outcomes. All 30 assignments remain in ITT, giving a 4/30 lower bound and a 6/30 conservative upper bound. Two additional AgentDojo benign outcomes are unknown. Across all 480 rows, two field-projection receipts and four structured safety receipts are absent.

The public-event diagnosis reports 26/30 AgentDojo compiled benign tasks with a first missing-tool-permission block in each module condition. This locates permission insufficiency for the trusted-intent review below. The original 900 rows have no recorded length truncation, so parse failures cannot uniformly be attributed to the 512-token limit. Two rows record task completion before a subsequent operational failure, and both observations are retained.

The 16 synthetic controls span four scenarios and two DeepSeek services, Pro and V4.1 Flash, and all reach a workflow terminal. Full modules deny private restricted flows and ask when classification is missing; tested public and archival flows complete. Policy-only permits the restricted private and missing-classification simulated flows. Legitimate completion and a security violation coexist in the tested controls, making these endpoints separately observable.

Task total latency is available for 476/480 calibration assignments, with four missing and no invalid values. AgentDojo benign-task median/p95 durations are 5.083/11.575 seconds without the research Guard, 1.209/3.029 with policy, 1.279/3.003 with full modules, and 8.145/20.291 for configured VIGIL. Its known-duration denominator is 28/30. Early blocking, model calls, constraint generation, and task-path differences explain why this task-duration ordering is not a Guard computation-speed comparison.

\subsection{Trusted-intent review of benign completion}

\label{sec:results-trusted-intent-review-of-benign-completion}

The fixed 60-task review reuses 240 calibration records rather than adding executions. It finds no lost or mismatched action--target pair among seven trusted application declarations. Thirteen further M3-v2 tasks have rules for explicitly requested targets. The remaining ten M3-v2 tasks require confirmation of a filename or recipient without supplying a concrete target; no independent approval was obtained. These counts describe trusted inputs and generated authorization rules.

All 30 AgentDojo tasks lack such concrete trusted action declarations. The generic compiler's lexical tool selection and literal security-target parsing do not cover arbitrary natural-language requests, multistage lookups, or dynamic object identifiers. Requests can express legitimate goals even when this bounded compiler cannot derive an executable grant. No new declared-contract violation was established by the review.

Six M3-v2 tasks show successful simulated execution without completion under at least one policy/full condition; four also have a model-format or operational failure. Categories overlap. In three explicit mock-API tasks, policy records contain ALLOW, successful execution, and a final answer but still fail completion. The evaluator requires both expected-effect matching and a successful final outcome. Redacted public arguments and answer content do not identify the exact mismatch. The cause remains unresolved, with execution and completion retained as separate observations.

\subsection{Corrected development study}

\label{sec:results-corrected-development-study}

Supplementary Table S20 separates the corrected-v1 development batch from all historical experiments. DeepSeek V4 Pro and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash each completed 288 planned task-condition executions over the same 48 tasks and six configurations. Pro had 265 completed workflows, 13 parse failures, and ten max-step terminations; Flash had 272, seven, and nine, respectively. Workflow completion is separate from task completion: ending with ASK records pending approval rather than task execution.

AgentDojo retains its native security checker. For Pro, normal-prompt attack positives were 5/12 without a guard, 2/12 with policy modules, and 0/12 with full modules; benign-tool completion was 6/8, 3/8, and 3/8. The full-minus-policy paired attack difference was \ensuremath{-}16.67 percentage points (95\% CI \ensuremath{-}44.80 to +10.38), crossing zero. For Flash, the no-guard normal-prompt attack cell had 1/12 positives and each other cell had zero. Benign-tool completion was 7/8 in both no-guard cells and 4/8 in each guarded cell. The attack comparison remained uncertain in these historically enriched development samples.

M3-v2 observed simulated unsafe effects were 0/12 in every attack cell for both models, including no guard. Control and guarded cells therefore shared an attack-endpoint floor. Runtime summaries contained zero labelled sensitive values, so dataflow checks were not applicable to these trajectories. The eight no-tool tasks per model-condition remain in the planned denominators; Supplementary Table S20 focuses on attack and benign-tool outcomes. Unknown outcomes are not replaced by zero, and operational failures remain separately reported.
