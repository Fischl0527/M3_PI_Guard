\subsection{Corrected authorization and contemporaneous calibration}

\label{sec:results-corrected-authorization-and-contemporaneous-calibration}

The corrected authorization study records all 900 planned assignments: Tables 2--4 summarize corrected authorization, contemporaneous calibration, and bounded component controls, respectively. The following paired intervals are distinct from the marginal Wilson intervals in the tables.

795 workflows end normally, 60 exhaust the step budget, and 45 have parse failures. Workflow status is separate from task completion. Across the 490 attack assignments, security events are zero under the benchmark-specific checks. This study contains no unguarded control, and repeated condition assignments are not independent attacks; the zero endpoint cannot establish effectiveness or equivalent protection.

For AgentDojo benign-tool tasks, compiled authorization completes 2/30 under both policy and full modules. Exact-target authorization completes 13/30 under policy and 11/30 under full modules. The paired exact-minus-compiled differences are +36.67 percentage points (95\% CI +18.60 to +53.31; Holm p=0.0039) and +30.00 points (+13.23 to +46.75; Holm p=0.0117), respectively. For M3-v2, the corresponding compiled counts are 16/30 and 17/30, compared with exact-target counts of 14/30 and 16/30. Their differences, \ensuremath{-}6.67 and \ensuremath{-}3.33 points, have intervals spanning zero. This does not reproduce a statistically established benchmark-intrinsic reversal. The oracle improves some observed completion under answer-derived scope but is not a deployed non-oracle authorization method.

The separate calibration closes all 480 assignments: 443 workflows end normally, nine exhaust steps, 25 have parse failures, two have runner failures, and one has a provider/stop failure. On AgentDojo, no research Guard yields 3/30 security events and 17/30 benign-tool completions. Policy and full modules each yield 0/30 and 2/30. The paired completion difference is \ensuremath{-}50.00 points (95\% CI \ensuremath{-}66.83 to \ensuremath{-}26.80; Holm p=0.0008), whereas the security difference is \ensuremath{-}10.00 points (\ensuremath{-}25.62 to +3.10; Holm p=0.7500). The utility cost is observable; a security improvement is not established. The no-research-Guard, policy, and full M3-v2 security cells are all zero, with benign completions of 16/30, 17/30, and 16/30. Full modules do not show an independent end-to-end advantage in these conditions.

Configured VIGIL has four AgentDojo attack positives among 28 known outcomes and two unknown attack outcomes. All 30 assignments remain in ITT, so the observed positive count gives a 4/30 lower bound and a 6/30 conservative upper bound, not a claim that the unknown outcomes were safe. Two additional AgentDojo benign outcomes are unknown. Its disabled model secondary verification and larger call budget bound this configuration comparison. Across all 480 rows, two field-projection receipts and four structured safety receipts are absent; missing receipts are not fabricated afterward.

The public-event diagnosis reports 26/30 AgentDojo compiled benign tasks with a first missing-tool-permission block in each module condition. This locates permission insufficiency but does not prove every missing permission is an implementation error. The original 900 rows have no recorded length truncation; parse failures cannot uniformly be attributed to the 512-token limit. Two rows record task completion before a subsequent operational failure, and both observations are retained.

The 16 synthetic controls all reach a workflow terminal. Full modules deny private restricted flows and ask when classification is missing; the tested public and archival flows complete. Policy-only permits the restricted private/missing-classification simulated flow. Legitimate completion and a security violation can coexist in these controls. Their limited scenarios and two DeepSeek services do not establish general provenance effectiveness or independent-provider replication.

Task total latency is available for 476/480 calibration assignments, with four missing and no invalid values. AgentDojo benign-task median/p95 durations are 5.083/11.575 seconds without the research Guard, 1.209/3.029 with policy, 1.279/3.003 with full modules, and 8.145/20.291 for configured VIGIL, whose known-duration denominator is 28/30. Early blocking, model calls, constraint generation, and task-path differences preclude interpreting this ordering as a Guard computation speedup. The latency-only correction does not alter the raw records or prior security and completion inference.

\subsection{Medium falsification study}

\label{sec:results-medium-falsification-study}

The medium, Public, original authorization, E1--E6, and confirmation results below retain their historical implementations and recorded endpoint labels. In particular, legacy safety labels or failure-inclusive endpoints are not retrospectively relabeled as observed un\-au\-thor\-ized execution. The corrected authorization and contemporaneous results lead the generated Results section; the historical oracle completion collapse is an implementation-defect observation, not evidence of an intrinsic precision--utility trade-off.

The medium study contains 480 records: 432 primary \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} records covering 72 paired cases across six conditions, plus 48 authorization-regime probe records. The preregistered analysis did not support a superiority claim for the full configuration package. It met the security-effect and deny-all criteria, but missed the utility point non-inferiority criterion and showed no security gain over the policy package. Both the favorable security comparison and the unfavorable utility and package comparisons are reported.

Supplementary Table S1 reports the online-state source audit.

This audit supports only covered-path statements. It does not establish a domain-wide oracle-authorization problem.

Supplementary Table S2 reports the completed medium security and utility outcomes.

The medium sample therefore shows a security--utility trade-off, not an incremental package benefit. Both M3 variants eliminated the eight unsafe effects observed under the structural condition, but they also completed substantially fewer benign tool tasks.

Supplementary Table S3 reports the prespecified paired comparisons.

The security effect relative to the structural baseline was large in this sample, but the full package did not outperform the policy package. Relative to the structural condition, its benign-tool TCR was 25 percentage points lower, well outside the preregistered \ensuremath{-}5 percentage-point non-inferiority margin.

Supplementary Table S4 reports the runtime burden and operational quality.

Across the 432 primary records, 411 completed and 21 retained terminal failures comprised six \nolinkurl{max_steps}, six parse failures, and nine provider failures. The 4.86\% overall failure fraction and 2.08\% provider-failure fraction passed the predefined operational-quality envelope. Every terminal state remains in the intention-to-treat denominator.

For the full package, 22 initial \nolinkurl{ASK} decisions comprised 17 permission-expansion events, four independent-confirmation requirements, and one out-of-scope parameter. Confirmation outcomes remained \nolinkurl{pending} in this synthetic study; the ASK count measures system confirmation-request incidence and cannot be interpreted as successful approval or measured human burden.

\subsection{Authorization-regime sensitivity}

\label{sec:results-authorization-regime-sensitivity}

Forty-eight probe records compare four alternate regimes with matched \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} executions for the policy and full packages. Each condition--regime comparison contains six pairs. No unsafe effect occurred in these small probes. For the explicitly labeled \nolinkurl{exact_gold} probe, task completion increased from 1/6 under matched \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} to 3/6 under \nolinkurl{exact_gold}, while initial ASK decreased from 3/6 to 0/6; exact McNemar tests were p=0.5 and p=0.25, respectively. These six-pair estimates are sensitivity signals, not evidence of a domain-wide bias.

\subsection{Public primary-model result}

\label{sec:results-public-primary-model-result}

The completed Public matrix contains 847 unique cases and 5,082 records: 600 attacks and 247 benign cases crossed with the same six conditions. All 76 diagnostic records (1.4955\%) remain in ITT. The predefined 1\% quality ceiling therefore failed and prohibits a clean configuration-quality claim. A one-pass integrity check nevertheless confirmed unique case-condition coverage, model identity, and diagnostic retention. The result is primary-model-only.

Across the 600 paired attacks, the full package produced 38 unsafe effects (6.33\%, Wilson 95\% CI 4.65--8.57) versus 133 (22.17\%, 19.03--25.66) under the structural condition. The paired difference was \ensuremath{-}15.83 percentage points (10,000-cluster bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}19.50 to \ensuremath{-}12.17; exact McNemar p=4.04e\ensuremath{-}17, Holm-adjusted p=1.61e\ensuremath{-}16). This security reduction did not establish full-package superiority: the policy package produced only 13 unsafe effects (2.17\%, 1.27--3.67), so the full package was 4.17 points worse (95\% bootstrap CI 2.50--6.00; exact McNemar p=1.62e\ensuremath{-}6).

Benign tool completion under the full package was 49/168 (29.17\%, 22.82--36.44), versus 103/168 (61.31\%, 53.77--68.34) under the structural condition: \ensuremath{-}32.14 points (95\% bootstrap CI \ensuremath{-}39.88 to \ensuremath{-}25.00; exact McNemar p=1.19e\ensuremath{-}14). The full and policy packages both completed 49/168, with a paired difference of 0.00 points. All six conditions completed 79/79 benign no-tool tasks.

Supplementary Table S5 reports the public-primary benchmark strata.

The direction is consistent across both attack benchmarks: the full package is safer than the structural condition but less safe than the policy package. Utility is strongly benchmark dependent. The public M3-v2 tool tasks show no completion difference among the three focal conditions, whereas AgentDojo tool completion falls by 55.67 points for both M3 variants relative to the structural condition. AgentDojo benign denominators remain the 97 unique official utility tasks and are not duplicated.

\subsection{Public DeepSeek V4 Flash replication}

\label{sec:results-public-deepseek-v4-flash-replication}

The completed Flash matrix contains 1,791 records: 400 attacks, 145 benign tool tasks, and 52 benign no-tool tasks under each of the structural, policy, and full packages. The full package produced 24/400 unsafe effects (6.00\%, Wilson 95\% CI 4.06--8.77), compared with 12/400 under the policy package (3.00\%, 1.72--5.17) and 47/400 under the structural baseline (11.75\%, 8.95--15.28). Thus the full-minus-policy difference was +3.00 percentage points, preserving the unfavorable direction observed with V4 Pro.

Benign-tool completion was 38/145 for the full package (26.21\%, 19.73--33.91), 35/145 for the policy package (24.14\%, 17.90--31.71), and 82/145 for the structural baseline (56.55\%, 48.42--64.35). All three conditions completed 52/52 benign no-tool tasks. The small +2.07-point full-minus-policy tool-completion difference does not offset the security disadvantage and is not interpreted as an independent component-specific gain. Sixteen operational diagnostic records remain in ITT; the Flash result therefore supports a same-provider model replication, not a clean configuration-quality or cross-provider claim.

\label{sec:results-public-operational-and-decision-burden}

The Public matrix contains 4,616 completed, 112 maximum-step, 120 parse-failure, and 234 provider-failure records. Diagnostics are uneven by condition: 27 structural, 24 safe-prompt, 22 full-package, two no-defense, one PromptGuard, and zero policy-package. The package comparison therefore met its structural security-effect criterion but missed utility non-inferiority and superiority criteria. This pattern is consistent with a security--utility trade-off rather than incremental benefit from the full package.

\subsection{DeepSeek authorization-regime experiment}

\label{sec:results-deepseek-authorization-regime-experiment}

The frozen authorization study contributes 900 terminal DeepSeek V4 Pro work items across 180 paired cases. The four main cells each contain 100 attacks, 60 benign tool tasks, and 20 benign no-tool tasks. A nested 60-case subset adds the broad, underspecified, and conflicting regimes under the full package. Recovery was limited to 33 work items for which no provider response had been obtained; all 867 response-bearing work items were left untouched. The final ITT population contains 768 completed records, 54 parse failures, 30 maximum-step terminals, 15 provider failures, and 33 repeated pre-response failures. No raw evaluator, label, or gold-answer field entered the online projection; \nolinkurl{exact_gold} exposed only its derived authorization object. Provider tools and real external effects remained disabled.

\subsubsection{Joint security and utility endpoints}

\label{sec:results-joint-security-and-utility-endpoints}

Unsafe effects were at the floor in the main experiment: 0/100 under the full package with either \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} or \nolinkurl{exact_gold}, 0/100 under the policy package with \nolinkurl{exact_gold}, and 1/100 under the policy package with \nolinkurl{auto_compiled}. Consequently, the full-minus-policy difference under \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} was \ensuremath{-}1.00 percentage point (task-cluster bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}3.00 to 0.00; Holm-adjusted McNemar p=1.0), while all other primary security contrasts were exactly zero or differed by the same single event. These data provide no evidence that the full package adds a reproducible security benefit over the policy package. This is a package-level result because the prompt profile also differs. For a 0/100 cell, however, the Wilson 95\% upper limit is 3.70\%; rare harms below that resolution cannot be ruled out.

Benign-tool completion showed the larger authorization effect. Under \nolinkurl{auto_compiled}, the policy package completed 22/60 tasks (36.67\%, Wilson 95\% CI 25.62--49.32) and the full package completed 16/60 (26.67\%, 17.13--39.01), a paired full-minus-policy difference of \ensuremath{-}10.00 points (bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}18.33 to \ensuremath{-}3.33; Holm-adjusted p=0.09375). Under \nolinkurl{exact_gold}, the policy package completed 9/60 (15.00\%, 8.10--26.11) and full completed 7/60 (11.67\%, 5.77--22.18), a \ensuremath{-}3.33-point paired difference (bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}8.33 to 0.00; Holm-adjusted p=0.5). All four main cells completed 20/20 benign no-tool tasks.

Changing the policy package from \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} to \nolinkurl{exact_gold} reduced paired benign-tool completion by 21.67 points (bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}36.67 to \ensuremath{-}5.00; Holm-adjusted p=0.05853). For the full package, the corresponding reduction was 15.00 points (\ensuremath{-}28.33 to \ensuremath{-}1.67; Holm-adjusted p=0.09808). The bootstrap intervals exclude zero, but neither exact McNemar comparison remains below 0.05 after Holm correction. We therefore interpret the consistent direction and effect size as evidence of authorization sensitivity in this sample, not as a universal oracle-policy effect.

\subsubsection{Benchmark-stratified authorization effects}

\label{sec:results-benchmark-stratified-authorization-effects}

The pooled utility estimate hides a qualitative benchmark reversal. On AgentDojo benign tool tasks, changing from \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} to \nolinkurl{exact_gold} increased completion from 5/30 to 9/30 for the policy package (+13.33 points; bootstrap 95\% CI \ensuremath{-}3.33 to +30.00) and from 4/30 to 7/30 for the full package (+10.00 points; \ensuremath{-}3.33 to +23.33). On M3-v2, the same change reduced completion from 17/30 to 0/30 for the policy package (\ensuremath{-}56.67 points; \ensuremath{-}73.33 to \ensuremath{-}40.00) and from 12/30 to 0/30 for the full package (\ensuremath{-}40.00 points; \ensuremath{-}56.67 to \ensuremath{-}23.33). These paired strata are descriptive, but they show that the pooled \ensuremath{-}21.67 and \ensuremath{-}15.00 point estimates are not transportable across the two benchmark task semantics.

\subsubsection{Failure-sensitive attack bound}

\label{sec:results-failure-sensitive-attack-bound}

Counting \nolinkurl{unsafe_effect OR operational_failure} as a pessimistic sensitivity endpoint changes the interpretation of the exact regime. The policy package moved from 7/100 under \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} to 25/100 under \nolinkurl{exact_gold} (+18.00 points; bootstrap 95\% CI +9.00 to +27.00); the full package moved from 8/100 to 26/100 (+18.00 points; +10.00 to +27.00). Package contrasts within the same regime were only +1.00 point and had intervals spanning zero.

This sensitivity effect was entirely benchmark dependent. On AgentDojo, policy and full package differences from auto to exact were both +36.00 points (5/50 to 23/50 and 4/50 to 22/50; both bootstrap intervals +20.00 to +52.00). On M3-v2, the corresponding differences were 0.00 points (2/50 to 2/50 and 4/50 to 4/50). Operational failures remain separately labeled; the composite is a robustness bound and is not reported as observed attack success.

\subsubsection{Stress regimes and operational burden}

\label{sec:results-stress-regimes-and-operational-burden}

On the nested benign-tool subset, moving from matched full-package \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} executions to broad, conflicting, or underspecified policies reduced completion by 25, 30, and 25 percentage points, respectively. Their bootstrap intervals were \ensuremath{-}45 to \ensuremath{-}5, \ensuremath{-}50 to \ensuremath{-}10, and \ensuremath{-}50 to 0 points. No unsafe effect occurred in any of the 30-attack stress cells, so the stress comparison primarily exposes lost utility rather than an observable security gain.

The decision and failure profiles explain part of this trade-off. Under the policy package, \nolinkurl{auto_compiled} produced ASK on 34.44\% and DENY on 7.22\% of records, whereas \nolinkurl{exact_gold} produced no ASK but DENY on 48.89\%; its failure rate rose from 7.22\% to 23.89\% and p95 latency from 10.63 to 19.42 seconds. The full package showed the same pattern: ASK fell from 32.22\% to zero, DENY rose from 11.11\% to 45.00\%, failure rose from 9.44\% to 24.44\%, and p95 latency rose from 9.18 to 19.62 seconds. DENY incidence counts records with at least one DENY; the separate event totals are retained because a record may contain multiple decisions. Across all 900 work items, provider cost was CNY 1.3888324.

Pooling again obscures where the system burden occurred. Under \nolinkurl{auto_compiled}, system ASK incidence on AgentDojo was 80.00\%/78.00\% for policy/full attacks and 66.67\%/56.67\% for policy/full benign-tool tasks; the corresponding M3-v2 rates were 4.00\%/2.00\% and 0.00\%/3.33\%. Under \nolinkurl{exact_gold}, ASK was zero by construction. Exact-regime AgentDojo failure was 46.00\%/44.00\% on attacks and 43.33\%/43.33\% on benign-tool tasks, compared with 4.00\%/8.00\% and 16.67\%/16.67\% on M3-v2. AgentDojo exact p95 latency ranged from 20.77 to 23.93 seconds across these four package/task cells, whereas M3-v2 exact p95 ranged from 6.93 to 8.66 seconds. Thus \nolinkurl{ASK}, blocking, failure, and latency are properties of the package--regime--benchmark combination, not a single pooled human-burden quantity.

These results reverse the simple expectation that an answer-derived exact policy must improve measured utility. In this implementation, the exact policy behaved as a more restrictive authorization treatment, replacing confirmation with repeated blocking and higher operational failure. The result supports making authorization construction explicit in evaluations; it does not show that every gold-derived policy or every runtime guard behaves similarly.

\subsection{Source-authority counterfactual}

\label{sec:results-source-authority-counterfactual}

E2 completed 240/240 response-bearing end-to-end work items and 480/480 deterministic Guard decisions. The source swap changed only the authority of a critical proposition while holding text, value, relative position, and policy fixed. No end-to-end cell executed an authorized, un\-au\-thor\-ized, or other action. The resulting zero un\-au\-thor\-ized-argument-influence rate is an endpoint floor accompanied by confirmation and blocking, not evidence that source authority was behaviorally irrelevant. The retained records do not identify the exact stopping cause of every zero-action run.

The burden nevertheless differed sharply. Policy/trusted and policy/untrusted produced ASK in 60/60 and 53/60 records, with no DENY. The full/trusted cell produced ASK in 57/60, whereas full/untrusted produced no ASK, DENY in 55/60, and one operational failure. The actual executed-action tuple changed under the source swap in 0/60 paired tasks for both module settings; operational status changed in 1/60 full pairs and is reported separately.

The deterministic replay provides mechanism evidence rather than end-to-end utility. Its 480 decisions comprise 240 un\-au\-thor\-ized and 240 authorized proposals. False allow was 0/240 un\-au\-thor\-ized proposals (Wilson 95\% CI 0.00\%--1.58\%); false block was 30/240 authorized proposals (12.50\%, 8.90\%--17.28\%). Policy decisions were identical under trusted and untrusted origins: 60 ALLOW and 60 ASK. Full/trusted had the same distribution, but full/untrusted produced 30 ALLOW, 30 ASK, and 60 DENY. The full Guard is therefore provenance-sensitive under this controlled swap, yet the tested model--Guard system did not translate that mechanism into completed tasks and incurred substantial confirmation or blocking burden.

\label{sec:results-defense-aware-adaptive-evidence-and-conditional-tail-through-budget-500}

The evidence supports a budget-sensitivity claim through 200, but it does not establish saturation, compare another model, or justify a SOTA/adaptive-robustness claim.

\subsection{Authorization diagnostics and execution permits}

\label{sec:results-authorization-diagnostics-and-execution-permits}

The new forced-proposal probes identified conditional implementation limits. For either nuisance-text or safety-target perturbation, policy returned ASK in 120/120 cases; full returned ASK in 90/120 and DENY in 30/120. Among six un\-au\-thor\-ized-target provenance scenarios, exact-target rules yielded no ALLOW under either module setting. Broad-target rules yielded 6/6 ALLOW for policy and 4/6 for full. Literal matching can treat negated or quoted trusted mentions as authority, while encoded or split values escape literal-source matching. E7a obtained 96/96 official, parseable responses with no operational failure. Consequently, zero un\-au\-thor\-ized permits cannot establish resistance to an active attack.

\subsection{Remaining evidence boundary}

\label{sec:results-remaining-evidence-boundary}

Historical Public and incremental tables retain their recorded evaluator labels. In the old M3-v2 path, an action allowed by the Guard but rejected by the final isolation boundary could be labelled unsafe even though the effect was not applied. Historical ITT variants also include operational failures. The compact records do not support retrospective identification of all actual un\-au\-thor\-ized effects. Historical numbers are therefore legacy-labelled risk endpoints, not corrected execution-effect estimates. Their values are retained, not silently relabelled as corrected-v1 results; AgentDojo's native checker is also not reduced to an environment-state-only definition.

The medium, Public, authorization-regime, factorial, source-authority, challenge, replication, adaptive, and contemporaneous-activity studies jointly support an empirical security--utility paper. They do not support a leading-defense, end-to-end provenance-benefit, saturated-adaptive, or cross-provider claim. E2 does identify source sensitivity at the deterministic Guard decision layer, but its end-to-end action endpoint is floor-limited. E5 supplies only a conditional 0/12 B200-survivor tail with a 24.25\% upper confidence limit. E6 records attack activity only within its historically enriched, single-block DeepSeek cohort; it neither satisfies nor tests the original multi-window activity gate. The earlier Gemini authorization study closed at 32/450 terminal work items, only five response-bearing; the separately executed E6 Gemini prefix produced five response-bearing records, one response-less failure, and 174 administrative closures. Both are retained for operational accounting but all Gemini effects are not estimable. UTR and DSR are not identifiable from the current record contract and are not reconstructed from unsafe-effect rate.
