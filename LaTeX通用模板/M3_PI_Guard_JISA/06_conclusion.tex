\section{Conclusion}

\label{sec:conclusion}

This study treats runtime authorization as an experimental variable rather than fixed infrastructure. Across the completed medium and Public studies, the full configuration package reduced unsafe effects relative to a structural baseline but did not improve on the policy package. The larger historical direction replicated across DeepSeek V4 Pro and V4 Flash: the full package produced more unsafe effects, while benign-tool utility was equal or only slightly different.

The prospectively frozen authorization experiment explains why security alone is an incomplete endpoint. Unsafe effects remained at or near zero in all four main cells. The pooled exact-minus-auto benign-tool differences were \ensuremath{-}21.67 points for the policy package and \ensuremath{-}15.00 for the full package, but AgentDojo improved by 13.33/10.00 points while M3-v2 fell by 56.67/40.00. Under a pessimistic unsafe-or-operational-failure bound, both packages worsened by 18 points overall, driven by +36-point changes on AgentDojo and no change on M3-v2. Exact authorization removed system ASK but moved 45.00--48.89\% of records into at least one DENY, raised failures to approximately 24\%, and increased p95 latency. Broad, conflicting, and underspecified regimes also reduced completion on the nested tool-task subset without changing the observed attack floor. Authorization construction can therefore materially alter the measured security--utility frontier even when an attack-only result looks stable.

The concurrent 2\ensuremath{\times}2 experiment then separated prompt profile from the runtime module bundle. Across 400 attack assignments, the policy cells produced two unsafe effects and the full cells produced none; the module estimate was \ensuremath{-}1.00 percentage point (95\% bootstrap CI \ensuremath{-}3.00 to 0.00), while the prompt and interaction estimates were zero. Benign-tool module and prompt effects were both \ensuremath{-}3.33 points with intervals including zero. The design resolves the prompt/module confounding but remains floor-limited and does not isolate the individual provenance, data-flow, monotonicity, or confirmation components.

The source-authority counterfactual supplies the corresponding mechanism evidence. Full-Guard decisions changed under an untrusted source, whereas the policy-only distribution did not; 480 replay decisions comprised 0/240 false allows among un\-au\-thor\-ized proposals and 30/240 false blocks among authorized proposals. Yet all 240 end-to-end runs executed no action because ASK or DENY intervened. Mechanism-level provenance sensitivity is therefore not equivalent to usable end-to-end utility. The outcome-enriched challenge set likewise reproduced only 1/60 historical unsafe effects and incurred 55 operational failures, while the Flash factorial replication had zero unsafe effects in every attack cell. Both results reinforce the need to report a failure-sensitive endpoint instead of treating non-operation as safety.

The adaptive panel supplies a related measurement warning. First unsafe effects were observed for 4/40 tasks by query 50 and 18/40 by query 200. A single low attack budget would have missed most successes found by the larger budget. Among the 12 prespecified B200 survivors, 0/12 produced a new first unsafe effect by query 500 (Wilson upper limit 24.25\%). This conditional result cannot be merged into a full B500 curve or used to establish saturation or zero tail risk.

A separately executed single-block calibration observed attack activity in a historically risk-enriched cohort: no defense produced 17/60 unsafe effects across both benchmarks, compared with 2/60 under policy/normal and 0/60 under full/safe. Because 47/60 tasks also anchored E3, this is not independent-sample replication. These conditional contrasts remain bounded by non-negligible operational failure, outcome-enriched sampling, the absence of benign tasks, and a single execution block. They do not satisfy the original multi-window activity gate or estimate deployment prevalence, utility, temporal repeatability, or an individual provenance-module effect.

These findings support four practical requirements for tool-agent security evaluation: keep evaluator-only fields outside online Guard state; manipulate prompt and runtime factors separately; pair deterministic source-authority replay with end-to-end execution; and report utility, ASK/DENY incidence, failures, latency, and attack budget alongside unsafe effects. The conclusions remain limited to the tested policies, benchmarks, runtime implementation, DeepSeek models, and budget range. Both the earlier Gemini authorization sample and the separate E6 Gemini prefix closed below their paired-coverage gates; all Gemini effects are not estimable and cannot erase or broaden the completed DeepSeek findings.
