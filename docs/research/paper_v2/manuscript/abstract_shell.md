## Background slot

Tool-using language-model agents must distinguish actions authorized by a
trusted task from actions induced by untrusted context. Existing evaluations
can conflate detector output, runtime authorization, executed effects, and the
authorization information exposed to an online guard.

## Method slot

We evaluate authorization regime and runtime enforcement using outcome-blind
frozen tasks, explicit online/evaluator state separation, executed
unsafe-effect and benign task-completion endpoints, and append-only
provider/effect evidence. The study directly pairs a structural condition,
M3 policy-only, and M3-full, and prospectively retains provider, parser, and
maximum-step failures in the intention-to-treat denominator.

## Admitted medium-result slot

In the admitted 72-case, 480-record DeepSeek medium study, M3-full reduced
unsafe effects from 8/36 under the structural condition to 0/36
(paired difference −22.22 percentage points; clustered-bootstrap 95% CI
−36.11 to −8.33). However, M3 policy-only also produced 0/36 unsafe effects,
and benign tool completion fell from 12/24 under the structural condition to
6/24 under M3-full. The preregistered mechanism gate therefore rejected the
mechanism-paper route.

## Admitted Public primary-result slot

In the admitted 847-case, 5,082-record Public primary-model matrix, M3-full
produced 38/600 unsafe effects versus 133/600 under the structural condition
(paired difference −15.83 points; 95% bootstrap CI −19.50 to −12.17), but
policy-only produced only 13/600. Benign tool completion was 49/168 under both
M3 variants versus 103/168 under the structural condition. All 76 diagnostic
records remained in ITT; their 1.4955% frequency exceeded the frozen 1%
mechanism-quality ceiling. The result therefore confirms a security–utility
frontier and negative module gain for one primary model, not cross-model or
clean-mechanism superiority.
