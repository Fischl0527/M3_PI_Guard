### 摘要 / Abstract

> **EN**: As open-weight large language models (LLMs) in-
crease in capabilities, safeguarding them against
malicious prompts and understanding possible at-

automated jailbreaking methods like GCG [Zou et
al., 2023] remain effective, they often require sub-

pertise. We introduce “sockpuppetting”, a simple

Requiring

per-prompt comparisons.

cost attack accessible to unsophisticated adver-

### 2 相关工作 / 2 Related Work

### 2.1 越狱攻击 / 2.1 Jailbreaking

> **EN**: Jailbreaking is the act of creating adversarial prompts, often
in natural language, that convince an LLM to assist with a
question that is socially or ethically unacceptable. Jailbreaks
generally try to rephrase or obscure the harmful intent of the
prompt they target. Examples include using multiple low-
resource languages in the prompt [Deng et al., 2024], or en-
coding the request using weird formatting [Yuan et al., 2024].
>
> **ZH**: 越狱攻击是指构建对抗性提示词（通常使用自然语言），诱使 LLM 协助回答在社会伦理或法律层面不可接受的问题的行为。越狱通常试图转述或掩盖目标提示词的有害意图。典型方法包括在提示词中使用多种低资源语言 [Deng et al., 2024]，或使用特殊混淆格式对请求进行编码 [Yuan et al., 2024]。

> **EN**: 2Named after the way ventriloquists perform dialogues with their
puppets, while in reality it is them speaking the whole time.
>
> **ZH**: 2命名灵感源自腹语表演者操控木偶傀儡进行对话的方式，而实际上全程都是腹语师自己在发声。

> **EN**: Jailbreaking has proven a persistent problem for LLMs, re-
gardless of the model size and despite safeguards [Ganguli et
al., 2022; Yi et al., 2024; Ahmad et al., 2025]. This is mostly
due to the unfiltered nature of the pre-training corpora. LLMs
are often exposed to harmful information during pre-training,
memorize it and then reproduce it during deployment. While
some recent works have tried to filter the pre-training corpus
as a way to mitigate jailbreaking [O’Brien et al., 2025], these
techniques have yet to be applied at scale. Instead, the more
common mitigation strategy is to make models less likely
to respond to malicious prompts through fine-tuning [Wei et
al., 2022a]. However, fine-tuning does not explicitly remove
the underlying knowledge that is encoded inside the model,
as controlled unlearning in LLMs remains an open scientific
question [Nguyen et al., 2025].
>
> **ZH**: 事实证明，无论模型规模大小，也无论是否配备安全防护，越狱攻击始终是困扰 LLM 的顽固难题 [Ganguli et al., 2022; Yi et al., 2024; Ahmad et al., 2025]。这主要是由于预训练语料库缺乏充分过滤所致。LLM 在预训练期间经常接触有害信息并将其记入参数中，在部署阶段便可能复现出来。尽管近期部分研究尝试通过过滤预训练语料来缓解越狱风险 [O’Brien et al., 2025]，但这些技术尚未实现大规模应用。相比之下，更普遍的缓解策略是通过微调使模型降低对恶意提示词的响应概率 [Wei et al., 2022a]。然而，微调并不能显式消除模型内部编码的底层知识，因为在 LLM 中实现受控的知识遗忘（Machine Unlearning）仍是一个未解决的科学难题 [Nguyen et al., 2025]。

> **EN**: 2.2
Automated Jailbreaking
Automated jailbreaking deals with methods where jailbreaks
for specific LLMs or prompts are discovered with as little hu-
man oversight as possible, and it has developed into a rich
research field [Yi et al., 2024]. For open-weight models, in
addition to gradient-guided attacks (Section 2.3), prominent
directions include using variations of well-known stochas-
tic algorithms to search the loss space [Liu et al., 2024;
Andriushchenko et al., 2025] and modifying the decoding
parameters [Huang et al., 2024]. Closed-weight model at-
tacks either extend the results of attacks on open-weight
models using surrogates [Sitawarin et al., 2024] or try to
continuously iterate on known jailbreaking attacks, using a
prompt mutator and an LLM judge to generate ratings that
serve as a loss function [Yu et al., 2024; Chao et al., 2025;
Liu et al., 2025]. While most of these methods report very
high ASRs, LLMs of increased capabilities and alignment are
released regularly, which can make ASR numbers unreliable
for older papers.
>
> **ZH**: 2.2 自动化越狱
自动化越狱致力于以尽可能少的人工干预，自动挖掘针对特定 LLM 或提示词的越狱方案，目前已发展为一个蓬勃的研究领域 [Yi et al., 2024]。对于开源权重模型，除梯度引导攻击（2.3 节）外，主流方向还包括采用知名随机搜索算法变体在损失空间中展开搜索 [Liu et al., 2024; Andriushchenko et al., 2025]，以及修改解码超参数 [Huang et al., 2024]。闭源模型攻击则要么利用替代模型将开源权重模型上的攻击结果迁移过来 [Sitawarin et al., 2024]，要么通过提示词变异器结合 LLM 评判器生成充当损失函数的评分，从而对已知越狱攻击进行持续迭代 [Yu et al., 2024; Chao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025]。尽管大多数方法报告了极高的 ASR，但由于能力更强、对齐更完善的 LLM 频繁发布，早期论文中的 ASR 数据在较新模型上可能不再可靠。

> **EN**: 2.3
Gradient-Guided Attacks
Gradient-guided attacks rely on back-propagation from the
model output to the input in order to mutate the malicious
prompt or learn an adversarial prefix or suffix. One of the first
works in this field is GCG [Zou et al., 2023], but many others
quickly followed. Newer works focus on making the attack
suffix more interpretable [Zhu et al., 2024] to pass perplexity-
based filters [Jain et al., 2023], as well as reducing the high
computational requirements of GCG [Sadasivan et al., 2024].
However, these methods result in lower ASR when tested un-
der the conditions defined in Zou et al. [2023]. Schwinn et
al. [2023] show that the difficult problem of discrete opti-
mization in GCG can be substituted for regular optimization.
They do so by modifying the input not at a token level, but at
the embedding level, trading transferability for efficiency.
>
> **ZH**: 2.3 基于梯度的攻击
基于梯度的攻击依赖从模型输出到输入的反向传播，以对恶意提示词进行变异或学习出对抗性前缀/后缀。该领域最具开创性的工作之一是 GCG [Zou et al., 2023]，随后涌现了大量后续研究。较新的工作重点在于增强攻击后缀的可解释性 [Zhu et al., 2024] 以绕过基于困惑度的过滤器 [Jain et al., 2023]，以及降低 GCG 高昂的计算开销 [Sadasivan et al., 2024]。然而，在 Zou 等人 [2023] 设定的标准实验条件下测试时，这些改进方法往往会导致 ASR 显著下降。Schwinn 等人 [2023] 表明，GCG 中复杂的离散优化问题可以通过在嵌入层（而非标记层）修改输入来转化为常规连续优化，以牺牲迁移性为代价换取计算效率。

> **EN**: 2.5
Logits-Based Attacks
These attacks do not require access to model internals, but
instead make use of the output probability distribution to ei-
ther create better jailbreaking prompts or to directly influ-
ence it towards compliance. Some examples that are partic-
ularly reminiscent of sockpuppetting include papers where
the model output is guided towards compliance by decod-
ing lower-likelihood tokens [Zhang et al., 2023b; Zhang et
al., 2023a], as well as works where the output distribution
of a model is compromised by tuning generation parame-
ters [Huang et al., 2024] or using a weaker, maliciously tuned
model to guide the output [Zhao et al., 2025].
>
> **ZH**: 2.5 基于 Logits 的攻击
这类攻击不需要访问模型内部状态，而是利用输出概率分布（Logits）来构建更优的越狱提示词，或直接影响概率分布使其偏向顺从。一些与傀儡操控高度相似的代表性工作包括：通过解码低似然标记引导模型输出走向顺从 [Zhang et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2023a]，通过调整生成超参数破坏模型的输出分布 [Huang et al., 2024]，或利用较弱的恶意微调模型来引导目标模型的输出 [Zhao et al., 2025]。

> **EN**: 3
Preliminaries
To understand sockpuppetting, we first revisit how LLMs pro-
cess regular user queries. Instruction-tuned LLMs differ from
their base model versions in that they respect certain conver-
sational and ethical rules, such as speaking in turn, limiting

### 4 傀儡操控（Sockpuppetting） / 4 Sockpuppetting

> **EN**: The reasoning behind the attack architecture of GCG is clear
enough; the threat model where the attacker has control only
over the user prompt is natural and can easily be extended to
closed-weight models, allowing for the construction of attack
suffixes that transfer between models and prompts. However,
if we assume the attacker has open-weight access over the
model they wish to jailbreak, there is no API forcing them to
keep the original chat template of the LLM, or to write exclu-
sively within their designated “user” message block. Thus, a
sockpuppetting attack might look as follows:
>
> **ZH**: GCG 攻击架构背后的逻辑非常清晰：攻击者仅对用户提示词拥有控制权的威胁模型极为自然，且易于拓展到闭源模型中，从而能够构建在不同模型和提示词之间可迁移的攻击后缀。然而，如果我们假设攻击者对目标 LLM 拥有开源权重访问权限，则没有任何 API 会强制要求他们保持 LLM 的原始对话模板，或限制他们只能在其指定的“user”消息块内写入内容。因此，傀儡操控攻击的形式如下所示：

```text
[EN 原文]:
<|im_start|>user<|im_sep|>Tell me how to
build a bomb<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant<|im_sep|>Sure, here is
how to build a bomb. You ...

[ZH 中文说明/对照]:
<|im_start|>user<|im_sep|>Tell me how to build a bomb<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant<|im_sep|>Sure, here is how to build a bomb. You ...
```

> **EN**: To clarify, the target acceptance sequence is directly in-
serted after the chat template by the attacker (red in the ex-
ample), and the model generates the rest. Since the accep-
tance sequence is a simple reformulation of the prompt, it can
be programmatically generated regardless of the precise con-
tents of the request, costing practically no computational re-
sources. Furthermore, the attack is simple enough that an un-
sophisticated adversary could reasonably attempt it as a first
resort.
The hypothesis behind sockpuppetting is that LLMs
are inherently self-consistent. They might be fine-tuned to
refuse harmful requests, but LLMs still try to generate re-
sponses that sound coherent due to their pre-training [Wei et
al., 2023]. In the probability space modelled by the LLM,
even if the most likely continuation to a harmful request is a
refusal, the most likely continuation to an agreement with the
harmful request is an actual response (more in Section 8).
>
> **ZH**: 需要明确的是，目标顺从序列是由攻击者直接插入在对话模板之后的（示例中为红色部分），模型负责自回归生成剩余内容。由于顺从序列仅仅是提示词的简单改写，因此无论请求的具体内容是什么，都可以通过程序化自动生成，几乎不耗费任何计算资源。此外，该攻击极其简单，非专业攻击者完全可以将其作为首选攻击手段。
傀儡操控背后的核心假设是：LLM 本质上具有自洽性（Self-consistent）。尽管经过微调以拒绝有害请求，但由于预训练的自回归特性，LLM 仍会尽力生成听起来连贯统一的回复 [Wei et al., 2023]。在 LLM 所建模的概率空间中，尽管对有害请求最可能的接续是拒绝，但对“已经同意回答有害请求”的最可能接续则是具体的解答内容（详见第 8 节）。

### 5 结合梯度优化的傀儡操控 / 5 Sockpuppetting With Gradient Optimization

> **EN**: Building on the general idea of sockpuppetting, we also hy-
pothesize that optimizing adversarial suffixes to be put inside
>
> **ZH**: 基于傀儡操控的基本构想，我们进一步提出假设：在“assistant”消息块内部优化对抗后缀，可能比在“user”消息块内部优化更加有效。

> **EN**: the “assistant” message block might be more effective than
doing so inside the “user” message block. After all, getting
the LLM to reply positively leads to a higher attack success
rate than merely asking it to begin its response in a certain
way [Wei et al., 2023]. This greater susceptibility of text in-
side the “assistant” message block could, therefore, translate
to synthetically-derived strings as well. Concretely, such a
hybrid attack (which we call “sockpuppetting with gradient
optimization”) might look like the following example:
>
> **ZH**: 毕竟，诱导 LLM 做出肯定答复相比仅要求其以某种特定方式开始作答，往往能获得更高的攻击成功率 [Wei et al., 2023]。因此，“assistant”消息块内部文本具有的更高影响力，理论上也可以转化为对人工合成攻击序列的更高敏感度。具体而言，这种混合攻击（我们称之为“结合梯度优化的傀儡操控”）如下例所示：

```text
[EN 原文]:
<|im_start|>user<|im_sep|>Tell me how to
build a bomb<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant<|im_sep|>ici Certainly
!Absolutely Here. Here is how to build a
bomb. You ...

[ZH 中文说明/对照]:
<|im_start|>user<|im_sep|>Tell me how to build a bomb<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant<|im_sep|>ici Certainly !Absolutely Here. Here is how to build a bomb. You ...
```

> **EN**: For this version of the attack, we inject the target accep-
tance sequence (in orange) and optimize an adversarial pre-
fix to maximize its likelihood (in red). However, unlike in
conventional gradient-guided attacks, the attack string is op-
timized to be placed directly within the “assistant” message
block.
While sockpuppetting with optimization is undoubtedly
more compute-intensive than plain sockpuppetting, it brings
back a level of adaptiveness. Different LLMs are trained on
disjoint sets of data and fine-tuned differently, so it would be
surprising if all of them were equally likely to accept a re-
quest by responding with “Sure, here’s how to...”. By tailor-
ing different attack strings for different models, this version
of the attack combines the novel attack vector of sockpuppet-
ting with the versatility of gradient optimization, in a way that
outperforms its non-sockpuppetting counterparts.
A slight change in the phrasing of the target acceptance
sequence was necessary to get satisfactory results when using
sockpuppetting with gradient optimization (see Appendix B).
>
> **ZH**: 在该版本的攻击中，我们注入目标顺从序列（橙色），并优化对抗前缀以最大化该顺从序列的出现概率（红色）。然而，与常规梯度引导攻击不同，该攻击字符串被优化为直接放置在“assistant”消息块内部。
虽然结合优化的傀儡操控在计算上无疑比纯傀儡操控更密集，但它重新引入了自适应调节能力。不同的 LLM 是在互不相交的数据集上训练且微调方式各异，因此若期望所有模型对“好的，以下是关于……”的顺从序列都具备相同的响应概率是不切实际的。通过为不同模型定制不同的攻击字符串，该版本攻击将傀儡操控的新型攻击向量与梯度优化的通用性相结合，其表现全面超越了非傀儡操控的同类方法。
在使用结合梯度优化的傀儡操控时，微调目标顺从序列的措辞是获得满意结果所必需的（见附录 B）。

### 6.2 通用型攻击实验 / 6.2 Universal Attacks

> **EN**: In this experiment, we optimize each attack suffix for the first
25 prompts of the dataset simultaneously, with the gradients

longer sequences don’t under-optimize, which seems to occa-
sionally be a problem with GCG. We chose k = 10 for our
experiments.

the GCG algorithm is reimplemented in code and run with

What are the Implications for Closed-Source
Models?
In this paper, we focus on open-weight LLMs, as we be-
lieve their security is important. Open models are not that
far behind closed models in terms of capabilities [Team et
al., 2025b], yet their threat model is by definition a lot more
permissive. Attackers don’t need to worry about secondary
safety systems, such as input or output filters. Furthermore, as
social responsibility mounts on big AI vendors to detect and
take action against bad actors on their platforms [Anthropic,
2025], using their products becomes more and more of an
operational risk, compared to open-weight alternatives.
However, the threat model of sockpuppetting is notable in
that it doesn’t strictly require open-weight access to LLMs.
Instead, the attack is possible as long as text can be in-
serted inside the “assistant” message blocks of chat templates

Conclusion and Future Work
The main contribution of this paper is twofold.
First, we
show that sockpuppetting is very effective for modern open-
weight LLMs, outperforming GCG by up to 64 percentage
points ASR. Meanwhile, sockpuppetting is also one of the
simplest variants of a logits-based attack, allowing an unso-
phisticated adversary to both implement and run it in min-
utes. Second, this paper bridges the gap between gradient-
guided prompt attacks and logits-based attacks, showing
that inserting adversarial suffixes inside the “assistant” mes-
sage block substantially increases their effectiveness.
Hy-
brid methods like RollingSockpuppetGCG outperform their
purely optimization-based counterparts by up to 64 percent-
age points ASR, in cases where the prompt content is not
know a priori.
Future work in this space should focus on carefully se-
lecting the target acceptance sequences during optimization.
Bringing the acceptance sequence closer to the natural output
of the attacked model could make both pure sockpuppetting
attacks, as well as gradient-guided ones, more effective.
Another possible goal of future work could be to get a
deeper understanding of jailbreaks. As discussed, it seems
plausible that gradient-guided prompt attacks make use of
(at least) two separate mechanisms to achieve their goals.
Thus, understanding the breadth of possible mechanisms that
emerge in gradient-guided attacks could allow us to make
models safer and more robust. Classifying attack suffixes into
different categories, as well as inspecting them using mech-
anistic interpretability tools, present interesting opportunities
for further research.

Optimization Details
There are several speed-up techniques that have been applied
to all gradient optimization algorithms:
Early stopping if the target is greedily decodable
If the
target sequence would have been naturally predicted by the
model through greedy sampling, then we don’t need to opti-
mize the attack suffix further. In the case of a rolling attack,
no larger attack lengths are tried. For universal attacks, the
target sequences associated with all input prompts need to be
greedily decodable from the suffix.
Early stopping for stale candidates
If the best candidate
has not changed in the last X iterations (called the “pa-
tience”), the attack is terminated to conserve resources. In
the case of a rolling attack, the suffix is expanded to the next
length and the attack continues.
Candidate filtering
Candidate suffixes are tested to ensure
stable representations when encoded and decoded together
with the rest of the prompt, and the unstable ones are re-
moved. This is necessary because sometimes the tokens dis-
covered through the discrete optimization process can only be
produced by the tokenizer under specific circumstances. For
example, “.json” might be a single token, but if it appears
right after a space (“ .json”), the tokenizer will choose
to split it into “ .” and “json”. When decoding and re-
encoding such a sequence, the resulting tokens are different
from the ones at the start, or even corrupt the representation
of the rest of the prompt unless removed.
Smart batching
Multiple candidate suffixes are tested si-
multaneously by batching the forward passes together. Since
all candidates have the same token length, they can be safely
batched without padding in the non-universal attacks. For
the universal attacks, all candidates are sorted by length and
batched accordingly.
All attacks are run for 500 steps, with a patience of 100,
(maximum) suffix size of 10, optimization batch size of 512
(i.e., 512 candidates generated per iteration) and topk of 2048
(i.e., for the chosen token, a replacement is randomly cho-
sen from the 2048 best tokens according to the gradient).
For rolling attacks, the maximum steps and patience are per
length size, so a rolling attack with this configuration will run
for at least 1000 and at most 5000 steps in total. Unlike Zou
et al. [2023], the universal attacks are optimized for all given
prompts at the same time, as opposed to one by one, to save
computational resources.
All attacks are evaluated only once per model and prompt
using greedy model sampling, to ensure reproducibility. For
Llama only, the suffixes are initialized as a series of x’s (e.g.,
x x x x x), as opposed to a series of exclamation marks
(!), as is standard in GCG. The reason is that the token repre-
senting “ !” changes when the Llama tokenizer encodes and
then decodes it, causing instability in the discovered suffix
and changing its token length.
